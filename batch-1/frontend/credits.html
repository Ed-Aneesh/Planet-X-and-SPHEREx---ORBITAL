01Required acknowledgements

SPHEREx. This work uses data from the Spectro-Photometer for the History of the Universe, Epoch of Reionization and Ices Explorer (SPHEREx), a NASA mission managed by JPL/Caltech, obtained from the NASA/IPAC Infrared Science Archive (IRSA), which is operated by the California Institute of Technology under contract with the National Aeronautics and Space Administration.
IRSA. This research has made use of the NASA/IPAC Infrared Science Archive, which is funded by the National Aeronautics and Space Administration and operated by the California Institute of Technology.
2MASS. This publication makes use of data products from the Two Micron All Sky Survey, which is a joint project of the University of Massachusetts and the Infrared Processing and Analysis Center/California Institute of Technology, funded by the National Aeronautics and Space Administration and the National Science Foundation.
WISE. This publication makes use of data products from the Wide-field Infrared Survey Explorer, which is a joint project of the University of California, Los Angeles, and the Jet Propulsion Laboratory/California Institute of Technology, funded by the National Aeronautics and Space Administration.
Gaia. This work has made use of data from the European Space Agency (ESA) mission Gaia (cosmos.esa.int/gaia), processed by the Gaia Data Processing and Analysis Consortium (DPAC, cosmos.esa.int/web/gaia/dpac/consortium). Funding for the DPAC has been provided by national institutions, in particular the institutions participating in the Gaia Multilateral Agreement.
CDS. This research has made use of Aladin Lite and the SIMBAD database, operated at CDS, Strasbourg Astronomical Observatory, France.

03Archives, services & documentation

codeNASA/IPAC IRSA Simple Image Access v2 service (irsa.ipac.caltech.edu/SIA) and TAP service (irsa.ipac.caltech.edu/TAP).Finding every SPHEREx exposure that covers a position; raced against each other so either can be slow.

codeIRSA image cutout service for SPHEREx spectral images; SPHEREx Archive User Guide: data products and data access.Downloading only the small patch we need from each 2040×2040 image.

codecheckSPHEREx Science Data Center, SPHEREx Explanatory Supplement (SSDC-DP-017), Caltech/IPAC, irsa.ipac.caltech.edu/data/SPHEREx/docs/SPHEREx_Expsupp_QR.pdf; IRSA DR1 overview and QR overview; PSF header error notice.File layout (IMAGE, FLAGS, VARIANCE, ZODI, PSF, WCS-WAVE), flag bits, units, release rules, why old PSF layers are refused.

codeIRSA, SPHEREx tutorials: intro, cutouts, PSF.How to query, cut out and read SPHEREx files; reading the PSF extension.

codeIRSA SPHEREx Data Explorer and Spectrophotometry Tool (irsa.ipac.caltech.edu/applications/spherex).“Every SPHEREx image here” buttons open IRSA's own tools with the search already run.

codeIRSA Moving Object Search Tool (MOST), catalog = spherex (irsa.ipac.caltech.edu/applications/MOST).Verifying which asteroids SPHEREx really photographed, and when (asteroid presets in the Sky Probe).

codeIRSA Gator catalogue service, table gaia_dr3_source (irsa.ipac.caltech.edu/cgi-bin/Gator).Gaia DR3 fallback when the TAP services are down (it was the only one answering on 15 Nov 2026).

codeIRSA 2MASS All-Sky Atlas and AllWISE Atlas image services (SIA collections twomass_allsky and wise_allwise).Older images placed on the SPHEREx grid.

codeESA Gaia Archive TAP and DataLink (gea.esac.esa.int/archive); ESA, How to retrieve DataLink products.First choice for Gaia DR3 astrometry; XP spectra fallback.

codeDemleitner, M. & Andrae, R., Gaia DR3 XP spectrum datalink / Monte-Carlo sampled XP spectra, GAVO Data Center, Heidelberg (dc.g-vo.org/gaia/s3).Gaia XP spectra with decorrelated errors, the version we fit in the SED.

codeNASA/JPL Solar System Dynamics, Small-Body Identification API (ssd-api.jpl.nasa.gov/doc/sb_ident.html).Which known asteroids and comets were in the patch on each SPHEREx date.

checkNASA/JPL Horizons ephemeris system (ssd.jpl.nasa.gov/horizons).Checking the units of the Small-Body API's motion rates (on-sky RA rate, arcsec per hour).

codeWenger, M. et al. 2000, The SIMBAD astronomical database, A&AS 143, 9 (simbad.cds.unistra.fr).Target positions, proper motions and object types; name look-up in the sky atlas.

codeBonnarel, F. et al. 2000, The ALADIN interactive sky atlas, A&AS 143, 33; Boch, T. & Fernique, P. 2014, Aladin Lite: Embed your Sky in the Browser, ASP Conf. Ser. 485, 277.The zoomable sky atlas (Aladin Lite 3.8.2, shipped with the site).

codeFernique, P. et al. 2015, Hierarchical progressive surveys, A&A 578, A114; HiPS mirrors at CDS, ESA ESASky, CSIRO CASDA and China-VO.Background sky images (2MASS, AllWISE, DSS colour) in the atlas, with mirrors in case one server is down.

04SPHEREx mission & calibration papers

contextBock, J. J. et al. 2025/2026, The SPHEREx Satellite Mission, ApJ (doi:10.3847/1538-4357/ae2be2; arXiv:2511.02985).102 channels, 0.75–5 µm, 6.15″ pixels, survey cadence, paired detectors: the facts behind our design.

contextCrill, B. P. et al., SPHEREx: NASA's Near-Infrared Spectrophotometric All-Sky Survey (arXiv:2404.11017); Doré, O. et al. 2014, Cosmology with the SPHEREx All-Sky Spectral Survey (arXiv:1412.4872).Mission design, release plan (weekly quick releases, yearly reprocessed releases, all-sky cube).

methodcheckHui, H., Bock, J. J., Condon, S. et al. 2026, Spectral Response of SPHEREx, ApJS 284, 10 (doi:10.3847/1538-4365/ae522c; arXiv:2602.09139).Linear variable filters, per-pixel wavelength and bandwidth, wavelength-calibration accuracy used in the Lab's spectral error note.

contextKorngut, P. M. et al. 2026, The SPHEREx Instrument: Calibration, testing and performance measurements (arXiv:2603.29835).Instrument behaviour (PSF, detectors, dichroic split at 2.42 µm).

contextAkeson, R. et al. 2026, The SPHEREx Image and Spectrophotometry Processing Pipeline, ApJS 285, 67.How the L2 images we use are made (zodiacal-light model, flags, calibration).

contextCukierman, A. J. et al. 2026, Spectral Map Making with SPHEREx, ApJS 286, 22 (arXiv:2603.25790).How spectral maps/cubes are built from single exposures: why the DR1 cube's effective PSF is broader than one exposure's.

methodcheckSPHEREx Ultracool Dwarf spectrophotometric Atlas (SUDA), 2026 (arXiv:2604.26771).The ~5 % absolute-calibration term in our photometry error budget.

methodcheckGagné, J., Faherty, J. K. et al. 2026, A SPHEREx Pipeline and Spectral Library for Ultracool Dwarfs (SPIFF) (arXiv:2604.22012).Forced ePSF photometry of fast movers at each epoch (same idea as our Lab); SPHEREx's 2 % calibration design goal and their 8 % conservative floor.

contextRustamkulov, Z. et al. 2026, SPHEREx 0.75 to 5 µm Spectra for a Sequence of Nearby Brown Dwarfs (arXiv:2607.00543).WISE 0855 only detected beyond ~3.9 µm (so we default it to D6); bad pixels under-flagged in early data.

05Methods papers we implemented

Positions, motions and Gaia

methodESA 1997, The Hipparcos and Tycho Catalogues, ESA SP-1200, Vol. 1, Sect. 1.2.8; Butkevich, A. G. & Lindegren, L. 2014, Rigorous treatment of barycentric stellar motion, A&A 570, A62.Rigorous 3-D epoch propagation with perspective acceleration (Barnard's Star). Verified against astropy/ERFA to < 0.1 mas.

methodLindegren, L. et al. 2021, Gaia Early Data Release 3: Parallax bias versus magnitude, colour, and position, A&A 649, A4.The −17 µas parallax zero point.

methodEl-Badry, K., Rix, H.-W. & Heintz, T. M. 2021, A million binaries from Gaia eDR3, MNRAS 506, 2269.Warning that Gaia errors of bright stars are underestimated.

methodBailer-Jones, C. A. L. et al. 2021, Estimating Distances from Parallaxes. V., AJ 161, 147.Why we refuse 1/parallax when parallax/error < 5.

methodSchönrich, R., Binney, J. & Dehnen, W. 2010, Local kinematics and the local standard of rest, MNRAS 403, 1829.Sun's motion (U, V, W) for space velocities relative to the local standard of rest.

methodGaia Collaboration, Gaia DR3 documentation, Sect. 4.1.7 (ICRS–Galactic transformation) (gea.esac.esa.int/archive/documentation/GDR3).The rotation matrix for U, V, W.

Images, maps and projections

methodGreisen, E. W. & Calabretta, M. R. 2002, Representations of world coordinates in FITS, A&A 395, 1061; Calabretta, M. R. & Greisen, E. W. 2002, Representations of celestial coordinates in FITS, A&A 395, 1077.WCS: turning pixel positions into sky coordinates (our numpy TAN/SIN code and the alignment of every frame).

methodShupe, D. L. et al. 2005, The SIP Convention for Representing Distortion in FITS Image Headers, ASP Conf. Ser. 347, 491.SPHEREx's optical distortion terms, so fixed stars stay on the same pixel.

methodGórski, K. M. et al. 2005, HEALPix: A Framework for High-Resolution Discretization and Fast Analysis of Data Distributed on the Sphere, ApJ 622, 759.Finding the DR1 cube tile for any position (nested pixel index).

methodCalabretta, M. R. & Roukema, B. F. 2007, Mapping on the HEALPix grid, MNRAS 381, 865.The HPX projection of the cube tiles (matches astropy to 6×10⁻¹² px).

methodTody, D. 1986, The IRAF Data Reduction and Analysis System, Proc. SPIE 627, 733 (ZScale algorithm); Lupton, R. H., Gunn, J. E. & Szalay, A. S. 1999, A Modified Magnitude System that Produces Well-Behaved Magnitudes, Colors, and Errors Even for Low Signal-to-Noise Ratio Measurements, AJ 118, 1406; Lupton, R. et al. 2004, PASP 116, 133.Display stretch: ZScale limits + asinh, shared by all frames so brightness changes stay honest.

methodAnderson, J. & King, I. R. 2000, Toward High-Precision Astrometry with WFPC2. I. Deriving an Accurate Point-Spread Function, PASP 112, 1360.The effective-PSF (ePSF) idea behind SPHEREx's PSF tables and our PSF-fitting photometry.

Spectra, statistics and time series

methodVaughan, S. et al. 2003, On characterizing the variability properties of X-ray light curves from active galaxies, MNRAS 345, 1271.Excess variance and fractional variability Fvar with errors.

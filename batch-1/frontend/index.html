In 1930 Clyde Tombaugh found Pluto by flipping between two photos of the same star field — a blink comparator. Thousands of stars stayed still; one dot jumped. ORBITAL does the same thing with modern data: it pulls every cutout SPHEREx has taken at your position, lines them up on the same WCS grid, and plays them in time order.

Moving dots = nearby stars and brown dwarfs with large proper motion.

Pulsing dots = variable stars whose brightness changes.

Everything else stays fixed — that's what makes the odd one out obvious.

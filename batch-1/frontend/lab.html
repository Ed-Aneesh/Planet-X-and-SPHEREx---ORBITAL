Science Lab · real SPHEREx data · every number with its error

Measure it, don't just watch it.

Blinking shows that something changes. The Lab measures how much, and how sure we are. Pick an object: the Lab finds every SPHEREx exposure of it at NASA/IPAC IRSA, measures its brightness in each one, builds its spectrum and light curve, and checks itself against Gaia and the DR1 all-sky cube.

New to this? Six words you will meet below
Photometry
Measuring how much light a star sends us. Here in millijansky (mJy): 1 Jy = 10−26 W m−2 Hz−1, the power per square metre per unit of frequency.
Aperture vs PSF
Two ways to count the light. Aperture: add up the pixels in a small circle, subtract the sky measured in a ring around it. PSF fitting: fit the known shape a point of light makes on the detector (the point-spread function, here SPHEREx's own measured "ePSF") — it weights each pixel by how much signal it carries, so it is less noisy.
SED
Spectral energy distribution: brightness against wavelength. A star's SED looks roughly like a blackbody curve whose peak sits at λpeak ≈ 2898 µm·K / T, so the shape tells us the temperature.
Light curve & periodogram
Brightness against time. The Lomb–Scargle periodogram tests every possible period and asks how well a sine wave of that period fits; a tall peak is a candidate period. The false-alarm probability (FAP) is the chance that pure noise makes a peak that tall.
Error budget
The list of everything that makes a number uncertain, and how much each contributes. Statistical errors shrink when you average many exposures; systematic ones (like the absolute calibration of the instrument) do not.
Parallax & proper motion
From Gaia (ESA). Parallax: the tiny yearly wobble as Earth orbits the Sun; distance in parsec = 1000 / parallax in milli-arcseconds. Proper motion: the star's steady drift across the sky, which we use to aim at the right spot on every date.
Bands

All six bands with 24 exposures each downloads ~150 small cutouts (≈15–30 MB) and takes a few minutes the first time; results of the same exposures are cached by the server afterwards.
